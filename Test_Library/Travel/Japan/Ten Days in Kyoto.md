# Ten Days in Kyoto（京都の十日間）

*Posted November 12*

Kyoto rewards slowness. I planned too much on day one and too little on day ten, which was perfect.

## Day by day

### Days 1–3: Higashiyama
Walked from Kiyomizu-dera (清水寺) down Sannenzaka at 7 a.m., before the crowds. The stone lanes were empty except for a shopkeeper sweeping. Breakfast was tamagoyaki and miso soup at a tiny counter that seated six.

### Days 4–5: Arashiyama
The bamboo grove is crowded by nine — go at dawn or skip it. The monkey park on the hill is worth the climb. Rented a bike and followed the river west.

### Days 6–8: Fushimi Inari and Nara
Hiked the full Inari loop (about 2.5 hours). Day-tripped to Nara for the deer, who are *extremely* polite until you hold a cracker.

### Days 9–10: Doing nothing
Coffee in the morning, a different temple garden every afternoon. Honestly the best two days.

## Things I'd tell a friend

1. Get an IC card (ICOCA) on day one.
2. Temples close early — most by 5 p.m.
3. Carry cash; small places still don't take cards.
4. Bring shoes you can slip off quickly.

## Long notes (scroll test)

The rest of this post is my unedited journal, kept here so the editor has something long to scroll through.

Morning one. Jet-lagged, awake at four. The city is quiet in a way big cities usually aren't. I walk until the convenience store lights are the only lights, buy an onigiri and a canned coffee, and watch the sky turn grey over the eastern hills.

Morning two. Rain. Every temple looks better in rain. The moss at the garden is impossibly green and the gravel raked into waves holds little puddles. An old man is drawing the pond in a sketchbook with a fountain pen and doesn't look up once in the hour I'm there.

Morning three. Found a coffee shop run by a man who roasts in the back room. He weighs every dose on a brass scale and pours like he's conducting. I order the house blend; he suggests the Kenyan instead and he's right.

Afternoon four. The bamboo is taller than I expected and the wind makes a sound like surf. A tour group arrives and the moment is gone, so I rent a bike and ride until the river widens and the houses thin out.

Evening five. Ate at a standing bar near the station. The menu is handwritten and I can read none of it; I point at what the person next to me is eating and it is wonderful.

Morning six. Fushimi Inari at sunrise. Ten thousand gates, and somewhere around the halfway point the crowds disappear and it's just me, the gates, and a cat asleep on a stone fox.

Evening seven. Nara. The deer bow. I bow back. We understand each other.

Morning eight. Slept in. First morning without an alarm in a year.

Days nine and ten. No notes. I was busy doing nothing, which turns out to be a skill.
