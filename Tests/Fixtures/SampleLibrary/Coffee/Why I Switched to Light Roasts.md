# Why I Switched to Light Roasts

*Posted March 3 · 4 min read*

For years I drank dark roast because that's what the café down the street served. Then a friend handed me a cup of a washed Ethiopian and it tasted like **blueberries and jasmine**. I was hooked.

## What changed

- Sweetness: light roasts keep more of the bean's natural sugars
- Acidity: bright, juicy — think citrus, not sour
- Origin character: you can actually taste *where* the coffee came from

> "Dark roast tastes like the roaster. Light roast tastes like the farm."
> — a barista in Portland

If you want to try it at home, start with my [pour-over guide](Brewing%20Guides/Pour-Over%20in%20Five%20Steps.md) and a medium-fine grind.

## Beans I've enjoyed

| Origin | Process | Notes | Rating |
|:--|:--|:--|--:|
| Ethiopia Yirgacheffe | Washed | Jasmine, bergamot | 5/5 |
| Kenya Nyeri | Washed | Blackcurrant, grapefruit | 4/5 |
| Colombia Huila | Honey | Caramel, red apple | 4/5 |
| Brazil Cerrado | Natural | Chocolate, nuts | 3/5 |

Light roasts are less forgiving[^1], so dial in slowly.

[^1]: They extract more slowly, so a grind that's too coarse gives you a sour cup.
